Our Story

Started in a garage. Still stubborn about it.

Kindling began in 2017 as a single drum roaster in a rented garage, roasting six pounds at a time for a Saturday farmers market table. Ten years later the drum is bigger, but the questions we ask a lot before buying it haven't changed: who grew this, how were they paid, and does it taste like the place it came from?

We still cup every new lot blind before it earns a spot on the shop page, and we still print the roast date — not a "best by" guess — on every bag.

Not Sure Where to Start?

How to pick a bag.

Roast level is the fastest filter. If you're switching from a grocery-store dark roast, land in the medium range first — a light roast can taste sour if you're not expecting the acidity.

If you like it bright

Try Highland Fog or Copper Valley. Best brewed as pour-over to let the fruit notes come through.

If you like it bold

Try Slow Ember or Midnight Kiln. Holds up well to milk and works great in a French press or moka pot.

Yirgacheffe, Ethiopia

1,900–2,200m · Washed · Oct–Dec harvest

Grown by a cooperative of around 400 smallholders on terraced hillsides. This lot is picked and pulped the same day, which is where the floral, tea-like brightness comes from. We've worked with this cooperative since 2019 and buy their entire allocation for our light roast program.

Huila, Colombia

1,600–1,850m · Honey · Apr–Jun harvest

A single family farm we've bought from since our first year — our longest-standing relationship. Honey-processed, meaning some of the fruit sugar stays on the bean during drying, hence the brown-sugar sweetness. The third generation now runs day-to-day picking.

Cerrado Mineiro, Brazil

1,000–1,200m · Natural · May–Aug harvest

Flat, sun-drenched plateau farming that produces a heavier, nuttier bean. Dried whole on raised beds for up to three weeks before hulling. This farm also runs a small nursery program that supplies seedlings to three neighboring smallholders.

Aceh, Sumatra

1,100–1,400m · Wet-hulled · Sep–Dec harvest

Processed using the regional giling basah method — hulled while still damp — which gives Sumatran coffee its low acidity and heavy, earthy body. Harvested by a collective of eleven households along the same ridge line.

Our Sourcing Promise

Three things we won't compromise on.

Pay above the floor

Every contract sits at least 15% above the Fairtrade minimum, and usually more — we publish the actual number on each origin's page.

Show up in person

Someone from the roastery visits every farm at least once a year, during or just after harvest.

Multi-year contracts

We commit to at least two harvests up front, so a farm isn't rebuilding a buyer relationship from scratch every season.

Brew Guide

A pour-over, five steps.

Works for any medium or light roast on this page. For dark roasts, drop the grind size slightly finer to compensate for the lighter bean density.

What You'll Need

· A dripper (V60, Kalita Wave, or similar)

· Paper filter, rinsed with hot water first

· A burr grinder — blade grinders make an uneven grind

· A gooseneck kettle, or any kettle you can pour slowly with

· A kitchen scale, ideally to the gram

· A timer — your phone works fine

01

Grind fresh

Medium-coarse, about the texture of kosher salt. Grind right before brewing — coffee loses most of its aroma within 20 minutes of grinding.

02

Bloom

Pour 60g of water (200°F) over 30g of grounds. Let it sit — you'll see it bubble as trapped CO2 escapes.

0:00 – 0:30
03

First pour

Pour in slow circles from the center outward, up to 250g total. Keep the water level steady rather than draining fully.

0:30 – 1:30
04

Second pour

Top up to 500g total, then let the bed drain fully. The grounds should look flat and slightly cratered when it's done.

1:30 – 2:45
05

Serve

Total brew time should land around 3:30. Swirl the carafe once and serve immediately — flavor flattens fast after this point.

~3:30 total

Coarse grind, 1:15 ratio, 4 minutes steep. Press slowly to avoid stirring up sediment.

Best for travel

AeroPress

Medium-fine grind, 1 minute steep, gentle press over 30 seconds. Forgiving and hard to mess up.

17g coffee · 250g water · 1:30

Best for milk drinks

Moka Pot

Fine grind, don't tamp. Pull off the heat the moment it starts sputtering to avoid a burnt taste.

Keeping It Fresh

Storage, in short.

Keep the bag sealed and in the cabinet — not the fridge or freezer, which introduce moisture on every open-and-close.

Coffee is best within 2–4 weeks of the roast date, and still good for a couple months after, just quieter.

Buy what you'll drink in that window rather than stocking up — freshness fades faster than the bag makes it look.
